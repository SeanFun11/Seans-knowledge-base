Events and private catering can bring in some of a food truck's biggest sales days. They also take more planning than a regular street service.

## Public Events

Festivals, fairs, concerts, and sporting events draw large crowds.

**What to expect:**
- An **application** and sometimes a vendor or booking fee
- Proof of **insurance** and **permits**
- Rules about menu, pricing, or setup times
- Long lines, so speed matters more than ever

> [!tip] Shrink the menu for big events
> Offer a reduced "event menu" of your fastest, best-selling items. Serving more people quickly usually beats offering more choices.

## Private Catering

Weddings, office parties, graduations, and neighborhood gatherings hire food trucks to serve guests.

| Pricing Model | How It Works |
|---|---|
| **Per person** | Host pays a set price for each guest |
| **Minimum guarantee** | Host guarantees a minimum sales amount; guests may pay |
| **Flat fee** | One price for a set time and menu |

## Booking Checklist

Before agreeing to any event, confirm:
1. Date, time, address, and setup time
2. Expected number of guests
3. Menu and pricing model
4. Parking surface and space for the truck
5. Whether power and water are available
6. Deposit and cancellation policy, **in writing**

## Planning Food Quantity

Estimate servings from guest count, then add a small buffer. Running out disappoints guests, but too much extra food cuts into profit. Coordinate prep with your commissary (see [[prep-and-commissary|Prep and Commissary]]).
