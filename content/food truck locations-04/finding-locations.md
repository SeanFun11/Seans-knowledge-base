For a food truck, **location is everything**. The same truck can have a record day in one spot and barely cover fuel in another.

## Types of Locations

| Location | Strengths | Challenges |
|---|---|---|
| **Office parks** | Steady weekday lunch crowd | Short service window; slow weekends |
| **Breweries and bars** | Evening customers; venue welcomes food | Sales depend on venue traffic |
| **Food truck parks** | Built-in crowds and amenities | Rent or fees; nearby competition |
| **Neighborhoods and apartments** | Loyal repeat customers | Must check HOA and parking rules |
| **Events and festivals** | Very high volume | Booking fees; see [[events-and-catering\|Events and Catering]] |

## What Makes a Good Spot

- **Foot traffic** at the times you'll be open
- **Visibility** from the street or sidewalk
- **Legal parking** that allows food sales
- **Room for a line** away from traffic
- **Few direct competitors** serving similar food nearby

> [!warning] Check the rules first
> Many cities limit where and how long food trucks can park, and some ban trucks near existing restaurants. Private property usually requires the owner's permission. Confirm before you set up (see [[permits-and-licenses|Permits and Licenses]]).

## Building a Weekly Route

Most trucks rotate through a regular schedule so customers know where to find them.

**Sample week:**
1. **Monday–Thursday lunch:** Two or three office parks in rotation
2. **Thursday evening:** Local brewery
3. **Friday and Saturday:** Festivals, events, or a food truck park
4. **Sunday:** Prep day or a private catering job

## Track Your Results

Record sales, weather, and customer count at every stop. After a few weeks, the data shows which spots are worth keeping.
