A **food truck park** is a dedicated lot where several food trucks park together, usually with shared seating and amenities. Parks have become popular gathering spots, especially in growing cities and suburbs.

## What Parks Usually Offer

- **Built-in customers** who come specifically to eat
- **Seating, shade, and restrooms** that a single truck can't provide
- **Electrical hookups** so trucks can turn off generators
- **Marketing** through the park's own social media and events
- Sometimes **drinks, music, or games** that keep people around longer

## Pros and Cons for a Truck Owner

| Pros | Cons |
|---|---|
| Steady foot traffic | Rent or a percentage of sales |
| Less time searching for spots | Direct competition a few feet away |
| Amenities customers enjoy | Park rules on hours, menu, or pricing |
| Easier permitting at a fixed site | Less freedom to move around |

## Questions to Ask Before Joining

1. What does it cost: flat rent, a share of sales, or both?
2. Which **days and hours** am I required to operate?
3. Are there **other trucks** with a similar menu?
4. Is **power** provided, or do I need my generator?
5. Does the park handle **trash and grease** disposal?
6. How does the park **promote** its trucks?

> [!tip] Stand out among neighbors
> In a park, customers compare trucks side by side. A clear menu board, an eye-catching wrap (see [[truck-wrap-and-branding|Truck Wrap and Branding]]), and fast service help you win the decision.

## Using Parks in Your Route

Many trucks treat a park as one stop in a weekly route rather than their only location. For example, a truck might serve office parks on weekdays and a food truck park on weekends (see [[finding-locations|Finding Locations]]).
