Where you buy food affects your **prices**, your **quality**, and your **reliability**. Most food trucks use a mix of suppliers.

## Common Supplier Types

| Supplier | Best For | Things to Know |
|---|---|---|
| **Broadline distributors** | Most ingredients and paper goods | Often require minimum order sizes |
| **Wholesale clubs and restaurant supply stores** | Flexible, smaller purchases | You pick up and transport the goods |
| **Local farms and farmers markets** | Fresh produce and specialty items | Supply may change by season |
| **Specialty vendors** | Bread, meats, or ethnic ingredients | Can set your truck apart |

## Choosing Suppliers

Ask each supplier:
1. What are your **delivery days** and minimum orders?
2. Can you deliver to my **commissary**?
3. How do you handle **substitutions** or out-of-stock items?
4. What are your **payment terms**?

> [!info] Approved sources
> Health codes require food to come from **approved sources**: licensed suppliers that follow food safety rules. Food prepared in a home kitchen generally can't be sold from a food truck.

## Receiving Deliveries Safely

- Check temperatures of cold and frozen items when they arrive
- Reject damaged packaging or food that looks or smells wrong
- Put cold items away **immediately**
- Label and date everything using **first in, first out (FIFO)**

## Managing Inventory

Small trucks have very little storage, so ordering the right amount matters.
- Track how much of each item you use per service
- Order based on upcoming events and weather
- Keep a short list of backup suppliers
