In a small kitchen that moves around, food safety takes extra attention. Limited space, outdoor heat, and long service hours all raise the risk of foodborne illness.

## The Four Steps

FoodSafety.gov organizes safe food handling into four simple steps:

1. **Clean:** Wash hands and surfaces often
2. **Separate:** Keep raw meat away from ready-to-eat food
3. **Cook:** Cook food to the right internal temperature
4. **Chill:** Refrigerate food promptly

## Temperature Control

Bacteria grow fastest between cold-holding and hot-holding temperatures, a range often called the **danger zone**. The FDA Food Code sets these key limits:

| Rule | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Cold holding | **41°F or below** |
| Hot holding | **135°F or above** |
| Poultry, cooked | **165°F** internal |
| Ground meats (like burgers), cooked | **155°F** internal |
| Reheating food for hot holding | **165°F** internal |

> [!warning]
> Your local health department may use slightly different numbers. Always follow the version of the code your area has adopted.

## Handwashing

Wash hands with soap and warm water:
- Before starting work and after breaks
- After handling raw meat
- After touching your face, phone, or money
- After taking out trash or cleaning

**Gloves do not replace handwashing.** Change gloves whenever you would wash your hands.

## Food Truck Challenges

- **Heat:** Truck interiors get hot; check refrigerator temperatures often
- **Limited water:** Plan water use so the handwashing sink never runs dry
- **Cash handling:** Money is dirty; separate the cashier from the cook when possible
