Your **concept** is the identity of your truck: what you serve, how it looks, and why customers should choose you over the truck parked next door. A strong concept is simple to explain in one sentence.

## Questions to Ask

- **What can I cook well, consistently, and quickly?**
- **Who are my customers?** Office workers, families, late-night crowds, event goers?
- **What's missing locally?** If there are already five taco trucks nearby, what makes yours different?
- **Can this food travel?** Some dishes don't hold up in a small kitchen or in a to-go box.

## Concept Checklist

A workable food truck concept usually has:

1. A **focused menu** of 5–10 items (see [[building-a-core-menu|Building a Core Menu]])
2. Food that can be made in **minutes**, not half an hour
3. Ingredients that **overlap** across dishes to reduce waste
4. A **name and look** that are easy to remember and photograph

## Researching the Market

Before committing, gather real information:

| Research Method | What It Tells You |
|---|---|
| Visit local food truck parks | What's popular and what prices customers accept |
| Check social media and review sites | What customers praise or complain about |
| Talk to event organizers | Which cuisines they need more of |
| Run a pop-up or test tasting | Whether people will actually pay for your food |

> [!tip]
> The U.S. Small Business Administration recommends studying your market and competitors before launching. See [[references|References]].
