Today's food trucks are part of a long tradition of selling food on wheels. Looking back at that history can inspire new owners and show how the business has always adapted to where people work and gather.

## Timeline

| Era | Development |
|---|---|
| **1866** | Texas cattle rancher **Charles Goodnight** converts an old Army wagon into the first **chuckwagon**, a rolling kitchen with shelves and drawers for feeding cowboys on cattle drives |
| **1872** | **Walter Scott** sells sandwiches, pie, and coffee from a horse-drawn wagon outside a newspaper office in Providence, Rhode Island, creating the **lunch wagon** |
| **Late 1800s** | Lunch wagons spread to other cities, and some later become stationary **diners** |
| **Mid-1900s** | Motorized lunch trucks serve **construction sites and factories**, and **ice cream trucks** roam neighborhoods |
| **Late 2000s** | **Gourmet food trucks** take off, using social media to announce their locations |
| **Today** | Food trucks serve almost every cuisine, cater weddings, and gather in **food truck parks** |

## The Chuckwagon

On long cattle drives, cowboys spent months away from towns. The chuckwagon carried food, cooking tools, water, and supplies so a cook could prepare meals on the trail. It's often called the ancestor of the food truck, and it started right here in **Texas**.

## The Lunch Wagon

Walter Scott noticed that night-shift workers had money to spend but nowhere to buy food after restaurants closed. His wagon solved that problem: bring the food **to** the customers. That idea is still the core of the food truck business today.

> [!quote] The lesson
> The most successful mobile food businesses go where the hungry people are, whether that's a cattle trail, a newspaper office, or an office park.

## The Modern Boom

The modern gourmet food truck movement grew quickly when:
- Social media let trucks share their **daily location** instantly
- Creative chefs saw trucks as a **lower-cost way** to start a food business
- Customers became excited about trying **new and fusion foods**
