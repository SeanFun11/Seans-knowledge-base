Because a food truck moves, customers need a way to know **where it will be today**. Social media is the main tool most trucks use to share their schedule and build a following.

## What to Post

| Post Type | Example | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Daily location** | "Serving at the Main St. office park, 11–2!" | Tells customers where to find you |
| **Weekly schedule** | A graphic with every stop this week | Helps customers plan ahead |
| **Food photos** | Close-up of the signature dish | Makes people hungry |
| **Behind the scenes** | Morning prep at the commissary | Builds a personal connection |
| **Specials** | "Limited time: spicy chicken sandwich" | Creates urgency |

## Tips for Better Posts

- Post your location **every day you operate**, at least a couple of hours before opening
- Use **real photos** taken in good light
- Keep captions short and include the **address and hours**
- Respond to comments and messages promptly
- Share posts from happy customers (with permission)

> [!tip] Consistency beats volume
> A simple, reliable daily location post does more for a food truck than occasional viral content.

## Beyond Social Media

- **Truck wrap and menu board:** Your truck is advertising wherever it parks
- **Online maps and review sites:** Keep business info and hours up to date
- **Email or text lists:** Reach loyal customers directly
- **Loyalty programs:** Reward repeat customers (see [[point-of-sale-and-payments|Point of Sale and Payments]])
