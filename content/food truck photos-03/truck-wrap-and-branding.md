A food truck is a **moving billboard**. Its look is often the first impression customers get, from across a festival field or in a photo shared online. Strong branding makes a truck easy to recognize and easy to remember.

## Elements of Food Truck Branding

| Element | What It Does |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Tells customers what you serve or sets the mood |
| **Logo** | A simple symbol people recognize instantly |
| **Colors** | Two or three colors used everywhere, consistently |
| **Truck wrap** | Turns the whole vehicle into advertising |
| **Menu board** | Shows the food clearly and matches the brand |
| **Social media profile** | Uses the same name, logo, and colors online |

## Designing a Truck Wrap

A good wrap is readable **from a distance** and **while moving**.

1. **Make the name huge.** It should be readable from across a parking lot.
2. **Say what you sell.** If the name doesn't make it obvious, add a short line like "Smoked BBQ" or "Street Tacos."
3. **Keep it simple.** Too many images and words turn into clutter.
4. **Use high contrast.** Dark text on light backgrounds, or the reverse.
5. **Include your handles.** Add your website or social media name so customers can find your schedule (see [[social-media-marketing|Social Media Marketing]]).

> [!info] Wraps vs. paint
> Vinyl wraps are popular because they can display detailed graphics and can be removed or replaced if the brand changes. Paint can last longer but limits design detail.

## Consistency Builds Recognition

Use the same colors, fonts, and logo on:
- The truck
- Menu boards and signs
- Packaging, cups, and stickers
- Social media posts and profile pictures

When everything matches, customers start recognizing the truck **before** they can read the name.

## Photos of the Truck

A well-branded truck makes great photos. Take pictures of it at different locations and events, in good light (see [[food-photography-basics|Food Photography Basics]]), and use them to show customers where you've been.
