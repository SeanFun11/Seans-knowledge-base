For a food truck, photos do the selling before a customer ever reaches the window. A good picture on a menu board or social media post can be the reason someone walks across the parking lot. You don't need expensive gear; a modern phone is enough if you understand a few basics.

## Light Is Everything

| Lighting | Result | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Soft natural light** | Food looks fresh and true to color | Shoot near the service window or outside in shade |
| **Direct midday sun** | Harsh shadows and shiny glare | Move into shade or wait for cloud cover |
| **Overhead kitchen lights** | Yellow or green color cast | Turn them off or step outside |
| **Phone flash** | Flat, greasy-looking food | Avoid it almost always |

> [!tip] Golden hour
> The hour after sunrise and before sunset gives warm, flattering light, which is great for photos of the truck itself.

## Composition Tips

1. **Pick one hero:** Make the signature dish the clear focus of the photo
2. **Choose the angle for the food:**
   - *Overhead (flat lay):* Bowls, pizzas, and spreads of several items
   - *Eye level:* Stacked items like burgers and sandwiches
   - *45 degrees:* Plates with height and texture
3. **Clean the edges:** Wipe sauce smudges off wrappers and containers
4. **Fill the frame:** Get close enough that the food, not the parking lot, is the subject
5. **Add context sometimes:** A hand holding the food or the truck in the background tells a story

## Make Food Look Fresh

- Photograph food **right after** it's made, before it cools or wilts
- Add a final garnish, like fresh herbs or a drizzle of sauce
- Show texture: melted cheese pulls, crispy edges, steam

## Basic Editing

Keep edits light so the photo matches what customers actually get:
- Adjust **brightness** and **contrast**
- Fix the **white balance** if colors look off
- Crop to remove distractions

> [!warning] Be honest
> Photos should represent the real portion and appearance. Over-edited pictures lead to disappointed customers and bad reviews.
