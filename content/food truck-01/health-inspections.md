A **health inspection** checks that the truck can store, cook, and serve food without making people sick. Most health departments base their rules on the **FDA Food Code**, which is adopted, sometimes with changes, by state and local governments.

## What Inspectors Commonly Check

- **Handwashing sink** with hot and cold water, soap, and paper towels
- **Water tanks:** separate fresh water and wastewater tanks of adequate size
- **Refrigeration** holding food at safe cold temperatures
- **Hot holding** equipment keeping food hot enough
- **Thermometers** available and used
- **Surfaces** that are smooth, durable, and easy to clean
- **Food storage** off the floor and protected from contamination
- **Employee practices** like hair restraints and glove use
- **Pest control** and clean conditions throughout

## Before the Inspection

1. Clean every surface, including under equipment
2. Test that all refrigeration units hold the correct temperatures
3. Fill the fresh water tank and empty the wastewater tank
4. Stock the handwashing sink
5. Have permits, food handler cards, and the commissary agreement ready

> [!tip] Think like an inspector
> Walk through the truck and ask, "Where could food get contaminated here?" Fix those spots before the inspector finds them.

## Common Violations

| Violation | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Food held at unsafe temperatures | Bacteria grow quickly in the danger zone |
| Handwashing sink blocked or unstocked | Workers can't wash properly |
| Raw meat stored above ready-to-eat food | Juices can drip and cross-contaminate |
| No working thermometer | Temperatures can't be verified |
