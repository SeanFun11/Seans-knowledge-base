Food trucks are regulated by several levels of government at once. Requirements differ from city to city, so the list below is a **starting checklist**, not a complete legal guide.

## Common Requirements

| License or Permit | Issued By | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Business registration** | State | Legally forms the business (LLC, sole proprietorship, etc.) |
| **Employer Identification Number (EIN)** | IRS | Federal tax ID for the business |
| **Sales tax permit** | State | Allows you to collect sales tax |
| **Mobile food unit permit** | City or county health department | Approves the truck to sell food |
| **Food handler / manager certification** | State-approved program | Proves staff know food safety rules |
| **Fire inspection** | Local fire department | Checks extinguishers, suppression, and propane |
| **Vehicle registration and insurance** | State DMV and insurer | Makes the truck street legal |

## The Usual Order of Steps

1. Register the business and get an EIN
2. Secure a **commissary** agreement (many health departments require one; see [[prep-and-commissary|Prep and Commissary]])
3. Complete food handler or manager training
4. Schedule the **health inspection** of the truck
5. Pass the **fire inspection**
6. Apply for the mobile food permit and sales tax permit
7. Check **zoning and parking** rules before choosing locations

> [!warning] Each city is different
> A permit from one city may not let you operate in the next town over. Check the rules everywhere you plan to serve, especially for events.

## Keeping Permits Current

- Keep copies of all permits **inside the truck**; inspectors and event organizers may ask to see them
- Put renewal dates on a calendar
- Update permits if you change the menu, equipment, or truck significantly
