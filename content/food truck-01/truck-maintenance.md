When a food truck breaks down, sales stop completely. Regular maintenance on **both the vehicle and the kitchen** protects your income and keeps you ready for inspections.

## Vehicle Maintenance

A food truck carries far more weight than a typical vehicle, which wears down parts faster.

| Item | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Oil and filter changes | Protects the engine under heavy loads |
| Tires and tire pressure | Extra weight increases wear and blowout risk |
| Brakes | Stopping a fully loaded truck takes more force |
| Battery and electrical | Keeps lights, pumps, and accessories running |
| Fluids and belts | Prevents overheating and roadside breakdowns |

## Kitchen Maintenance

### Daily
- Clean the grill, fryer, and hood filters
- Empty wastewater and refill fresh water
- Check refrigerator temperatures

### Weekly
- Deep clean surfaces and under equipment
- Filter or change fryer oil as needed
- Inspect propane lines and connections

### Periodically
- Have the **fire suppression system** professionally inspected
- Clean the **exhaust hood** and ductwork to remove grease buildup
- Service the **generator**

> [!warning] Grease is a fire hazard
> Built-up grease in the hood and filters is a leading cause of kitchen fires. Keep it on a regular cleaning schedule (see [[fire-and-propane-safety|Fire and Propane Safety]]).

## Keep a Maintenance Log

Record every service, repair, and inspection with the date and cost. A log helps you:
1. Spot recurring problems early
2. Budget for upcoming repairs
3. Prove maintenance to inspectors or future buyers
