A food truck kitchen may be only a few feet wide, so every inch has to work. A good layout lets the crew move quickly without bumping into each other or crossing raw and cooked food.

## Design Around the Workflow

Food should move in **one direction**, from storage to the customer:

1. **Storage:** Refrigerators and dry goods
2. **Prep:** Cutting boards and assembly space
3. **Cooking:** Grill, fryer, or flat top under the hood
4. **Finishing:** Plating, wrapping, and garnishing
5. **Service window:** Hand-off to the customer

> [!info] Why direction matters
> When food flows in one direction, raw ingredients don't cross paths with finished plates, which reduces cross-contamination (see [[food-safety-basics|Food Safety Basics]]).

## Layout Tips

- Put the **cooking line under the ventilation hood** and fire suppression system
- Place the **handwashing sink** where workers can reach it easily, not blocked by equipment
- Keep the **cash or POS station** near the window and away from food prep
- Store **heavy items low** so they don't shift while driving
- Secure every appliance and shelf so nothing moves in transit

## Common Layout Problems

| Problem | Result | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Fryer next to the sink | Water splashes into hot oil | Add a splash guard or separate them |
| One narrow aisle for two cooks | Slow service and accidents | Assign stations so each person stays in place |
| Refrigerator far from the line | Extra steps every order | Use under-counter refrigeration at the line |
