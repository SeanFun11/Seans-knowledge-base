Food trucks combine open flames, hot grease, propane, and generators in a very small space. The **National Fire Protection Association (NFPA)** publishes a food truck safety fact sheet summarizing the requirements in its codes.

## General Safety Rules

According to the NFPA fact sheet, food trucks should:

- Keep at least **10 feet of clearance** from buildings, other vehicles, and combustible materials
- Keep fire hydrants and fire department access routes **clear**
- **Never** allow public seating inside the truck
- Have the right **portable fire extinguishers** for the cooking being done
- Use an approved **fire suppression system** over cooking equipment that produces grease-laden vapors

## Propane Safety

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Secure tanks **upright** so they can't tip | Store tanks loose or lying down |
| Know where the **shutoff valve** is | Block access to the shutoff |
| Check connections for leaks regularly | Ignore the smell of gas |
| Keep **records of leak tests** in the truck | Refuel near open flames |

> [!danger] If you smell gas
> 1. Turn off all appliances and the propane supply
> 2. Get everyone away from the truck
> 3. Call the fire department
> 4. Do not restart anything until the leak is found and fixed

## Generator Safety

- Place generators so exhaust blows **away** from customers and the truck's service window
- Turn generators **off** before refueling
- Store fuel away from cooking areas and ignition sources

## Train the Crew

Every worker should know how to:
1. Use a fire extinguisher
2. Shut off the fuel supply
3. Call for help and give the truck's location
