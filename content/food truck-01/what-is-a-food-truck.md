A **food truck** is a motor vehicle equipped with a commercial kitchen that prepares and sells food directly to customers. Unlike a traditional restaurant, it can move to wherever the customers are: office parks at lunch, breweries in the evening, and festivals on the weekend.

## Food Truck vs. Other Mobile Food Units

Not every mobile food business is a truck. Local health departments often group them into categories:

| Type | Description | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Food truck** | Self-driven vehicle with a full kitchen | Cooking full meals on site |
| **Food trailer** | Kitchen towed behind another vehicle | Longer stays at one location |
| **Food cart** | Small, often pushed or towed unit | Simple items like hot dogs or ice cream |
| **Pop-up tent** | Temporary booth with portable equipment | Farmers markets and short events |

## How the Business Model Works

A food truck makes money the same way a restaurant does, by selling food for more than it costs to make. The difference is in the costs:

- **Lower overhead:** No building lease, and a much smaller staff
- **Higher flexibility:** The truck can follow demand and test new markets
- **Added challenges:** Weather, parking rules, vehicle breakdowns, and limited storage space

> [!info] Key idea
> A food truck is both a **restaurant** and a **vehicle**. Owners have to manage food costs and customer service *and* fuel, maintenance, and traffic laws.

## A Typical Day

1. Prep food at a licensed kitchen (see [[prep-and-commissary|Prep and Commissary]])
2. Load supplies, fill water tanks, and check propane
3. Drive to the service location and set up
4. Serve customers during the rush
5. Clean, dump wastewater, and restock for tomorrow
