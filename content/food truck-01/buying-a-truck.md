Choosing the vehicle is usually the biggest single decision, and the biggest single expense, in starting a food truck. See [[startup-costs|Startup Costs]] for how it fits into the overall budget.

## Your Main Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **New custom-built truck** | Built to your menu; reliable; warranty | Highest cost; long build time |
| **Used truck with kitchen** | Lower cost; ready sooner | May need repairs; layout may not fit your menu |
| **Food trailer** | Usually cheaper; no engine to maintain | Needs a tow vehicle; harder to maneuver |
| **Lease or rent** | Low upfront cost; test the business first | Higher long-term cost; less control |

## Inspecting a Used Truck

A used truck can be a great deal or an expensive mistake. Check **both** sides of the vehicle:

### The Vehicle
- Engine, transmission, brakes, and tires
- Rust on the frame and floor
- Mileage and maintenance records

### The Kitchen
- Do all appliances, refrigerators, and the hood fan work?
- Is there a working **fire suppression system**?
- Are water tanks, pumps, and the water heater in good shape?
- Will the layout pass inspection where *you* operate?

> [!tip] Get a second opinion
> Have a mechanic inspect the vehicle, and ask your local health department to review the kitchen *before* you buy. Rules differ by area, and a truck that passed in one county might not pass in yours.

## Questions to Ask the Seller

1. Why are you selling?
2. When was the fire suppression system last serviced?
3. Has the truck passed a health inspection recently, and where?
4. What repairs have been done in the last year?
